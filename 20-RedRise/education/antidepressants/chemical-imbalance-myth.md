# The Chemical Imbalance Myth

For decades, the "chemical imbalance" theory was presented as fact. It is not.

---

## Where the Theory Came From

- In the 1960s, it was observed that antidepressants affected serotonin levels
- This led to the hypothesis that depression was caused by low serotonin
- The theory was heavily marketed by pharmaceutical companies without solid evidence

---

## Why the Serotonin Hypothesis Is Flawed

- **No reliable biomarker** – there is no test to measure serotonin levels in the living brain
- **Conflicting evidence** – many studies found no correlation between serotonin levels and depression
- **The placebo effect** – many antidepressants barely outperform placebo in clinical trials
- **The "honeymoon" effect** – the initial feeling of relief often wears off, requiring dose increases

---

## What Research Actually Shows

Recent large-scale studies, such as a 2022 meta-analysis published in *Molecular Psychiatry*, found no consistent evidence that serotonin levels are lower in people with depression. The chemical imbalance theory is not supported by the data.

---

## Why the Myth Matters

- It gives people a false narrative for their suffering
- It discourages them from exploring root causes (environment, lifestyle, trauma, nutrition)
- It creates a model that "medication is the only solution," making withdrawal seem impossible

---

## Key Quote – Dr. Josef Witt-Doerring

> "The study is flawed… it showed that when you rapidly pulled people off the medication, there was more depressive relapse, which really it wasn't. It was just withdrawal."
