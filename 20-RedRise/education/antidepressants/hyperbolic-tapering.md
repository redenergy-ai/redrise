# Hyperbolic Tapering – The Gold Standard

Hyperbolic tapering is the evidence‑based approach to safely discontinuing antidepressants.

---

## Why Linear Tapering Fails

- Standard clinical guidelines often recommend reducing the dose by a fixed amount, e.g., 25mg every 2–4 weeks
- This is called **linear tapering**
- It fails because the relationship between dose and receptor occupancy is **not linear**

---

## The Receptor Occupancy Curve (Simplified)

- At low doses, a small decrease causes a large drop in receptor occupancy
- This means the worst withdrawal symptoms often occur at the very end of the taper
- This is known as the "cliff effect"

---

## The 10% Rule

**Reduce by 10% of your current dose every 2–4 weeks.**

- Example: If you are on 20mg, reduce to 18mg, then 16.2mg, then 14.6mg, etc.
- This accounts for the hyperbolic curve
- It prevents the "cliff effect"

---

## Why This Takes Months or Years

- The brain needs time to readjust between reductions
- Rushing increases the risk of severe withdrawal
- Slow tapering has a success rate of **over 90%**
