# Withdrawal vs. Relapse – How to Tell the Difference

One of the most common mistakes in psychiatry is mistaking withdrawal symptoms for a relapse of depression or anxiety.

---

## Why Withdrawal Is Often Misdiagnosed as Relapse

- The symptoms overlap (low mood, fatigue, irritability)
- Doctors are often untrained in withdrawal management
- Drug companies have historically downplayed withdrawal

---

## Key Differences

| Symptom | Withdrawal | Relapse |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Onset** | Rapid (24–72 hours after dose reduction) | Gradual (weeks to months) |
| **Duration** | Usually resolves within 2–4 weeks (if dose is held) | Persists indefinitely |
| **Physical symptoms** | Common (brain zaps, nausea, dizziness, paresthesia) | Rare |
| **Quality** | Often described as "not normal," "chemical," "foreign" | Feels like a return of the original condition |
| **Response to restarting** | Symptoms resolve rapidly | Symptoms improve gradually or not at all |

---

## Key Quote – Dr. Josef Witt-Doerring

> "If you stick a bunch of people on this drug for six months and then you stop it immediately or within two weeks, they are going to develop withdrawal symptoms. That will look like a depression."

---

## What to Do

- **If you suspect withdrawal:** Discuss it with your prescriber. Consider holding your dose or reinstating at a low dose.
- **Do not assume relapse:** Many people have been told they need to be "back on medication for life" when they were actually suffering from withdrawal.
