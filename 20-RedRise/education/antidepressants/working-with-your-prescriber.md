# How to Talk to Your Prescriber About Tapering

Having a productive conversation with your doctor about tapering can be challenging. Here's how to prepare.

---

## Before the Appointment

- **Educate yourself** – read the *Maudsley Deprescribing Guidelines*, watch Outro Health videos
- **Write down your goals** – what do you want to achieve?
- **Prepare your questions** (see below)

---

## Questions to Ask

| Question | Why Ask |
| :--- | :--- |
| "Are you familiar with hyperbolic tapering?" | Many doctors are not. This starts the conversation. |
| "Can we use liquid formulations?" | Allows for precise reductions. |
| "What is the smallest dose available?" | Standard pills often don't allow for slow tapering. |
| "How long do you recommend for my taper?" | If they say 2–4 weeks, they are not up to date. |
| "What should I do if I experience withdrawal symptoms?" | A plan prevents crisis. |

---

## What to Do If Your Doctor Dismisses Your Concerns

- **Stay calm and respectful**
- **Bring evidence** – print out *Maudsley Deprescribing Guidelines* or Outro Health resources
- **Ask for a second opinion** – seek a prescriber who understands withdrawal

---

## Finding a Tapering‑Friendly Prescriber

- Look for psychiatrists who specialize in deprescribing
- Ask in online communities (e.g., Inner Compass Initiative forums)
- Consider private practitioners who offer longer appointments

---

## Practical Strategies

| Strategy | Why It Helps |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Liquid formulations** | Allows for 1mg increments or less |
| **Compounding pharmacies** | Custom doses tailored to your taper |
| **Pill splitters** | Useful for splitting tablets (but limited) |
| **Slow, symptom‑led taper** | Safety first, timeline second |
